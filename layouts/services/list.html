Problem-first by design

You do not need to know which service you need before we talk.

Most worthwhile projects cross boundaries. A new website may expose a broken intake process. A reporting problem may actually be a data-structure problem. A brand project may need production standards and digital infrastructure. DCD starts with the outcome and works backward.

[01] “Our brand looks inconsistent everywhere.”

That may lead to identity reconstruction, guidelines, production standards, digital assets, and vendor-ready files.

[02] “Our website does not actually do anything for us.”

That may become a new site, intake flow, content system, analytics layer, portal, or connected customer experience.

[03] “We keep doing the same work manually.”

That may become Apps Script, API integration, routing logic, document generation, reporting, or exception-driven workflows.

[04] “We have data, but cannot use it.”

That may become a structured data layer, dashboards, extraction, classification, research tooling, or decision support.

[05] “Our tools do not talk to each other.”

That may require integration architecture connecting forms, email, files, databases, APIs, dashboards, and operational systems.

[06] “We know the outcome we want, but not what to build.”

That is a valid starting point. Discovery exists to determine the smallest useful system that can solve the real problem.

Questions

What working with DCD actually means.

The most important thing is not arriving with a perfect technical specification. It is being able to describe what is not working and what a better outcome would look like.

Do I need to know which of the four capabilities I need?

No. Start with the problem, current tools, constraints, and desired outcome. The discovery process determines whether the solution belongs in one capability or requires several.

Can DCD work with the systems we already use?

Often, yes. A major part of the systems-first approach is deciding what should stay, what should connect, what should be automated, and what actually needs to be replaced.

Is every project an AI project?

No. AI is one capability, not the default answer. If deterministic rules, better data, a simpler workflow, or ordinary software solves the problem more reliably, that is the better build.

Can one project expand later?

Yes. DCD intentionally favors reusable foundations: structured data, clean architecture, documented workflows, production-ready assets, and interfaces that can support future extensions.

Do you only work in healthcare?

No. Healthcare is a major specialization, but DCD also applies the same systems-first method to motorsports and sports, research and public information, professional services, entertainment, legal and record-intensive matters, and other organizations with custom operational problems.
